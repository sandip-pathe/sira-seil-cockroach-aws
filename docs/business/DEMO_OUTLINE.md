# Three-minute demo outline

## Research conclusion

Jack & Jill's original founder demo does not present two unrelated feature tours. Jack first learns the candidate and finds matching roles. He then hands to Jill, who learns the company and role. The two compare notes and end on a mutual introduction.

The public evidence does not confirm an exact 90-second split. The useful pattern is **two journeys, one outcome**. For Seilnsara, we will deliberately use 90 seconds for SIRA and 90 seconds for SEIL, with the transaction completing the handoff.

## Demo rule

Use one fictional buyer request and one continuous story. Do not stop to explain architecture, Stackfile, scoring weights, database state, or every possible decision outcome.

Persistent label: `Fictional company and products | sandbox`

## 0:00-1:30 — SIRA represents the buyer

### 0:00-0:15 — Start with the need

**Show:** The buyer asks SIRA for meeting-intelligence software for a ten-person client-services team.

**Prove:** SIRA begins with a real business job, not a product search query.

### 0:15-0:45 — SIRA learns the company

**Show:** SIRA asks focused questions about users, client-data policy, existing tools, budget, integrations, and who can approve the purchase.

**Prove:** The same request can produce a different answer for a different company.

### 0:45-1:05 — SIRA creates the buyer brief

**Show:** A concise requirement brief and a clear disclosure boundary: private context versus information allowed to reach sellers.

**Prove:** SIRA represents the buyer and controls what leaves the buyer workspace.

### 1:05-1:30 — SIRA finds products and hands off

**Show:** SIRA identifies relevant products and sends the permitted brief to their SEILs.

**Handoff:** Switch from the buyer workspace to the product workspace without changing the scenario.

## 1:30-3:00 — SEIL represents the product

### 1:30-1:55 — SEIL learns the product

**Show:** Seller-approved positioning, jobs to be done, capabilities, limitations, pricing, and terms. Show the sources behind the brief: product material, web evidence, reviews, and approved company input.

**Prove:** SEIL is a product representative, not a generic sales chatbot or copied landing page.

### 1:55-2:15 — SEIL answers the buyer

**Show:** Only the product facts relevant to SIRA's brief. Keep claims, independent evidence, limitations, and unanswered questions visibly separate.

**Prove:** SEIL can make a strong case without pretending every product fits every buyer.

### 2:15-2:30 — Honest `PASS`

**Show:** One candidate's SEIL returns `PASS` because it cannot support the required client-data workflow.

**Prove:** Seller-side honesty saves both sides from a bad sales cycle.

### 2:30-2:45 — The agents compare notes

**Show:** Back in SIRA, the buyer sees the surviving product, decisive evidence, trade-offs, and exact offer.

**Prove:** SEIL gets a voice; SIRA keeps the buyer's vote.

### 2:45-3:00 — Complete the deal

**Show:** The buyer approves the exact offer. Prava creates bounded authority. A changed merchant or increased amount is blocked; the approved sandbox path proceeds.

**End frame:** SIRA represents the buyer. SEIL represents the product. Together they move the right deal forward.

## What must be visible

- Distinct SIRA and SEIL workspaces
- Company context changing the questions or result
- A permitted buyer brief rather than raw private context
- Seller claims and independent evidence shown separately
- One honest `PASS`
- SIRA making the final recommendation
- The exact approved offer becoming Prava authority
- Sandbox and fixture labels throughout

## What to cut first if time runs long

1. Extra company-context fields
2. More than two product candidates
3. Detailed evidence browsing
4. The unsafe-payment mutation, if the approved sandbox completion is stronger and already certified

Never cut the SIRA-to-SEIL handoff or the final comparison. They are the product.
